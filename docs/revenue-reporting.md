# USD reporting and retention from confirmed terms

Open **Revenue** (`/app/revenue`) while signed in. The report is a projection of person-confirmed recurring contract versions, not a provider-reconciled billing report. Native invoice snapshots never stand in for subscription ARR. A source exception never becomes a revenue adjustment.

Owners/admins publish a named rate table. Fixed corporate rates cover an explicit inclusive 365- or 366-day fiscal year. Daily rates are manually recorded for one UTC date, naming European Central Bank reference rates or Open Exchange Rates; Buoy does not fetch or verify them. Each row names `currency`, `from`, `to`, `usdPerUnit` (decimal text, up to 12 fractional places) and `source`. USD is intrinsically 1; non-USD periods cannot overlap, even at their endpoints. Every publish requires the current table revision, retains its author/time/version and writes `revenue.rates-published` to the audit. Publishing preserves all native records and invalidates pending account approvals.

`GET /api/buoy/revenue?on=YYYY-MM-DD` shows native ARR/MRR beside USD figures with rate, period, source, table name and version. Conversion uses exact integer arithmetic and each ISO currency's minor-unit exponent, rounding half up to USD cents. MRR converts the exact annual amount divided by twelve. Missing rates produce null USD figures, never an inferred zero or a cross-currency sum. Current fields `revenue.usdArrMinor` and `revenue.usdMrrMinor` are explicitly USD-cent inputs to segments and saved account views; native `revenue.arrMinor`/`revenue.mrrMinor` continue to require an exact native-currency predicate. Rate edits recalculate current dependent segments before a successful response returns.

`GET /api/buoy/revenue/retention?period=quarter&end=2026-06-30` returns native-currency groups, constant-USD totals, FX movement, exact retention numerators/denominators, account movements, retained source versions and exceptions. `period` is `month`, `quarter` or `ttm`. End dates are observed UTC month ends; quarter ends are March, June, September and December. TTM covers twelve calendar months. The starting basis is the UTC day before the first date of the period.

At each boundary, the last confirmation dated on or before that date supplies the terms. Same-date confirmations sort by recording instant and then version ID. An active term includes its end date. Only accounts with positive starting ARR enter the starting cohort. NRR is ending cohort ARR divided by starting ARR. GRR caps each retained account's ending ARR at its starting ARR; expansion never offsets contraction or churn. Logo retention counts positive ending ARR among starting logos. New business and reactivations appear separately and do not enter retention numerators. A reactivation has a prior positive confirmation before the period, zero starting ARR and positive ending ARR. An absent denominator is unavailable, not 0%.

USD expansion/contraction/churn use the starting rate for both boundary amounts. The difference between ending-rate USD ARR and constant-rate ending ARR is FX only, including new and reactivated logos. Starting ARR plus expansion, minus contraction and churn, plus new business, reactivation and FX equals the selected portfolio’s ending ARR; retention numerators still exclude new/reactivated logos and FX. Rate edits restate the report while keeping earlier rate versions. Currency changes inside a period require source review; affected native totals and the USD total are unavailable. Missing rates preserve native figures and make USD totals unavailable. Sources, rate versions and current membership filters are read in one database transaction.

Optional `segmentId`, `ownerId` and `tier` filters use current segment membership, CSM and tier. They are not historical membership claims. `plan` selects an exact product present at either boundary; `cohort` selects the earliest recorded contract start month (`YYYY-MM`). Every reported account links to its native contract history. Account erasure removes its source versions and report rows. The rate table and history restore with the installation archive.
