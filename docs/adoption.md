# Adoption and purchased seats

Open **Usage → Product adoption metrics**. An owner or admin defines a feature or module with a stable key and an exact list of event names, and records purchased seats for each account. Saving the same kind/key replaces its event set. This is a measurement configuration, not a license verification or billing source. A blank seat count removes the denominator; zero is a real configured count. For a count, record the human source reference, observation date and 1–365 UTC-day freshness threshold (30 by default). The report labels this manual evidence as stale only after that many days; stale evidence remains visible beside utilization. A refresh or clear advances the account revision, invalidates earlier pending approval, and retains the old evidence with actor/time in seat history. Changing a threshold does not invent a new observation date.

The report and CSV use the reviewed USAGE-001 mappings and current customer memberships. Unreviewed claims and group-only observations never invent an active person. A person appearing under multiple reviewed source identities is one active person, including in a segment or portfolio. Distinct event deliveries still count as distinct activity; replaying the same source/message ID does not.

Choose a **Through UTC date**. Daily, weekly and monthly active users are distinct customer people with a `track` or `event` observation in the trailing 1, 7 or 30 inclusive UTC calendar days. `identify` and `group` messages are not activity. Each count compares with its own immediately preceding window of the same length. For example, through October 1, the current weekly period is September 25–October 1 and the previous week is September 18–24. Feature and module adoption is the number of monthly active people using that set divided by monthly active people. Overlapping event sets do not multiply an individual set's adopting users.

All other period metrics use 30 days:

- Depth is the number of active events divided by monthly active people.
- Breadth is the number of configured feature/module sets used divided by all configured sets. It is unavailable when no sets exist.
- License utilization is monthly active people divided by purchased seats. It may exceed 100%. A missing account denominator makes the aggregate unavailable; zero total seats also makes it unavailable. For a person, every selected explicit account membership needs a recorded license status and at least one must be licensed. This gives one individual seat, irrespective of multiple licensed memberships. Monthly activity is 0 or 1, so individual utilization is 0% or 100%; missing evidence or no licensed seat makes it unavailable. Individual assignments do not change account purchased-seat totals.
- Weekly change is `(current WAU − previous WAU) / previous WAU`. A decline of at least 30%, including exactly 30%, is flagged. A zero previous count produces an unavailable trend, not an inferred decline.

Time to first value uses each person's first retained, currently attributed `identify` observation and the first strictly later active event belonging to any configured feature or module. The report gives the number of people with both and their mean elapsed seconds, rounded to milliseconds. Without either observation it is unavailable. This is not a verified signup date or proof of business value.

Reports are available for a person, account, current segment or selected portfolio. Filters intersect. Current definitions, memberships, reviewed mappings and seats apply to both periods; the report does not claim historical segment membership or historical license entitlements. Installation coverage and source-reference counts describe the full current/previous 30-day window (60 days), separately from filtered metrics. Retained data never proves that every source delivered every event.

The authenticated API has the same report at `GET /api/buoy/adoption`, with optional `on`, `accountId`, `personId` and `segmentId` filters. CSV uses `/api/buoy/adoption/report.csv` with the same filters. Owners/admins write event sets at `POST /api/buoy/adoption/definitions` and seats at `PUT /api/buoy/adoption/seats`. These reads use existing staff account-read authority, while configuration requires the existing owner/admin authority and browser writes require the exact installation Origin. Source credentials and agent grants confer no adoption-report authority.

Definitions and seat counts persist in the installation database and archive. Account erasure removes its seat denominator and attributed observations. No new outbound provider is called. The reports are observations, not predictions or evidence of causation. ADOPT-001 remains partial: segment aggregate metrics do not yet drive configurable health, alerts or playbook triggers. Account and related-person metrics drive the health and alert measures described below and existing segment-entry proposals; adoption-driven at-risk accounts also support the existing reviewed single-task playbook. The original 90-day/30-account metric acceptance is asserted in `tests/adoption.mjs`; native configuration, filters, CSV, refusal/recovery and accessibility are asserted at 1440 and 390 in `tests/adoption-browser.mjs`.


In **Segments**, use numeric fields such as `adoption.current.utilization`, `adoption.previous.wau`, `adoption.current.feature.reports`, `adoption.weeklyChange` or `adoption.firstValue.meanSeconds`. Feature/module suffixes are the configured stable keys; fractions are 0–1 ratios, not percentages. All metrics use today's inclusive UTC report windows. Missing denominators are null, and only `is-missing` matches them. Related-person fields use `people.user.adoption.*` or `people.contact.adoption.*`; all conditions for a kind must hold for one person. Segment aggregates cannot feed membership rules. A definition change invalidates pending account proposals. A matching segment entry prepares one playbook proposal, and a person still approves it; a rule exit, changed definition, mapping, source observation or seat evidence refuses old approval. The public HTTP and native journeys are `tests/adoption-segment-journey.mjs` and `tests/adoption-segment-browser.mjs`.

In **Record an individual license**, choose an existing customer person and one of their account memberships, then record **Licensed** or **Not licensed** with a manual source reference, observed UTC date and 1–365-day freshness threshold (30 by default). This records the owner/admin’s evidence, never provider verification. **Clear evidence** removes the effective status and retains the old/new observation, actor and time in individual license history. Staleness begins strictly after the threshold and stays visible without discarding the ratio. A threshold edit does not alter the date unless explicitly supplied. Current status applies to both comparison periods. Filters select the memberships before calculating the denominator.

The same write is `PUT /api/buoy/adoption/person-licenses` with `accountId`, `personId`, `licensed` (`true`, `false` or `null` to clear), and, for a status, `reference`, `observedAt` and optional `staleDays`. It uses the existing owner/admin and Origin boundary. Refresh or clear advances every current membership account revision, so earlier proposals cannot be approved. Name edits and unchanged memberships retain evidence; removing a membership removes its effective license. Account erasure removes its individual evidence and history; archives retain current evidence and history. `tests/adoption-license-journey.mjs` and `tests/adoption-license-browser.mjs` assert these public doors and the native end states; `tests/portable.mjs` asserts the archive round trip.


In **Create an adoption measure**, choose an account metric or the metric of any current product user/contact, a threshold, and health, alert or both effects. All report numeric fields are available, including current/previous feature/module fractions and first-value seconds. Fractions use ratios: 0.9 means 90%. Comparisons are exact finite numbers; **Is missing** requires a blank threshold. There are at most 32 measures; disable an existing measure to remove its effects. Editing requires its current revision and invalidates pending account work. Every save is audited with an opaque measure ID.

A health match adds an at-risk reason. An unavailable required input adds unknown unless another input already shows risk; missing denominators never become zero. A related-person measure evaluates each current person independently and matches if any matches; an account with no such person has unknown input. Health reads, segment and outcome reports share the measure values and source/seat/license evidence. Source freshness and manual evidence remain labeled; retained data never proves complete delivery. Current UTC values evaluate on reads and after source/configuration changes. Later runway health bands carry today’s adoption evidence forward, without forecasting future usage.

An alert routes to the current active account owner’s inbox. Without an owner there is no recipient. Email follows that person’s existing signal preference: immediate, digest-only or no email. Read actual provider outcomes in **Notifications**; Buoy does not claim delivery. A rule/account/recipient receives one alert per matching episode; known recovery, disablement or reassignment rearms it, while unknown input alone does not count as recovery. Alerts evaluate after successful staff mutations and hourly; an intake observation can therefore wait until the next such evaluation. No Slack or Teams connection is implied.

The public staff doors are `GET /api/buoy/adoption/consumers` and owner/admin `POST /api/buoy/adoption/consumers`. A write uses `name`, `scope` (`account`, `user`, `contact`), `metric` (the full `adoption.*` key), `op` (`lt`, `lte`, `eq`, `gte`, `gt`, `is-missing`), numeric `value` (null only for is-missing), and boolean `health`, `alert`, `enabled`; at least one effect is required. An edit also includes `id` and `revision` from the current read. Existing session/Origin and agent refusal rules apply. Measures and alert episode state persist in archives; account erasure removes its episode state and inbox messages. As the backup guide states, restore establishes a fresh native owner. Explicitly reassign restored accounts to a current active person before expecting their alerts; the previous recipient’s private inbox does not transfer to that new identity. A newly assigned recipient gets one matching alert, and unchanged evidence does not duplicate it.
