# Buoy contract

Target edition: 17 October 2026. Buoy is a connected customer success platform for a US head of customer success at a B2B subscription or recurring-service company (roughly 5 to 50 customer-facing people, 100 to 10,000 accounts) replacing Gainsight CS, ChurnZero, Totango or Planhat, with the account workspaces, AI capture and agent execution buyers now see in Vitally and Velaris. Money is USD in integer cents, dates and schedules follow US time zones and daylight saving, and the first integrations are the US services those buyers already run: Salesforce, HubSpot, Stripe, Chargebee, Zendesk, Intercom, Segment, Snowflake, BigQuery, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Slack and Microsoft Teams.

Buoy does the whole post-sale job those products are bought for. It joins live CRM, billing, support and product-usage data per account and per user. It scores health in a way a CS lead can configure and explain. It turns risk and opportunity signals into automated playbooks and journeys that people and agents carry to a verified outcome. It runs onboarding projects and shared success plans with the customer, captures two-way email, meetings and chat alerts, and keeps a renewal and expansion pipeline whose NRR and GRR reconcile to billing. A buyer of any named incumbent can move their accounts, history, scorecards, playbooks and plans in and switch.

Sibling products keep their markets and are linked, never rebuilt: [Orbit](https://github.com/just-every/run-orbit) is the sales CRM that wins the deal and hands the account over (its INTEGRATE-008); [HappyToHelp](https://github.com/just-every/run-happytohelp) is the help desk; [Nectar](https://github.com/just-every/run-nectar) is subscription billing; [Pencil](https://github.com/just-every/run-pencil) owns general forms and research surveys; [Penny](https://github.com/just-every/run-penny) owns the product feedback board; [Vector](https://github.com/just-every/run-vector) owns issue tracking; [Mango](https://github.com/just-every/run-mango) owns marketing email; [Dial](https://github.com/just-every/run-dial) owns booking links; [Inkwell](https://github.com/just-every/run-inkwell) and [Locket](https://github.com/just-every/run-locket) own signature and contract records; [Nook](https://github.com/just-every/run-nook) is team chat; [Auth](https://github.com/just-every/run-auth) is the workforce identity provider; [Crank](https://github.com/just-every/run-crank) is general workflow automation. Each such seam below is a Buoy integration obligation, not a merged product. No gap in implementation, provider approval or partner agreement reduces this destination; it is recorded as a clause and built.

This is a target contract, not a release verdict. Every named clause states the behaviour a buyer gets and its acceptance evidence, the journey or test through a public door that proves it. The status table at the end marks each clause done, partial or missing against the code and tests on `main` at `4c6f2ee`; a passing local test does not establish hosted operation, live provider receipt or certification. Existing clause IDs are kept. This round changes no homepage, interface or runtime behaviour.

## Existing guarantees

- **HTTP-001 — Authenticated product HTTP.** Base handles identity and setup; Hono owns authenticated product HTTP handlers. Every data handler checks current identity, role and write origin before reading or writing. Agent credentials reach only the read and preparation commands their scope grants (GOVERN-001). **Acceptance evidence:** Signed out, cross-origin, as a member and as an agent token, call every product route; unauthenticated reads return 401, cross-origin writes 403, members cannot change accounts or approve, and agents can only use the preparation commands.

- **DATA-001 — Provenanced, validated observations.** Every account observation (billing, support, usage, CRM, survey or manual) carries its source system, source reference and observed time. Money is integer cents that cannot go negative, dates are real calendar dates, and unsupported fields are refused. Missing and stale evidence stays visible beside the value it qualifies, with a staleness threshold set per source (30 days for manual snapshots by default). No observation is silently overwritten: a refresh creates a new account revision. **Acceptance evidence:** Create and refresh accounts with valid, negative, impossible-date, missing and stale billing and support observations through the account door; invalid input is refused, missing and stale evidence is named on the account, and the earlier revision remains referenced by earlier work. Repeat with a connected source (CONNECT-001) once it exists.

- **APPROVAL-001 — Revision-bound human approval.** A proposal, whether prepared by a person, a playbook or an agent, snapshots the account revision it was based on. Approval re-checks that revision in one atomic D1 batch, records the approving person and time, and creates exactly one task, decision or approved action. A replayed approval changes nothing. Completion needs separate evidence. A source refresh invalidates pending approvals and never rewrites earlier decisions. This is the gate every sensitive step in PLAY-001, EMAIL-002, RENEW-001 and the agent clauses uses. **Acceptance evidence:** Prepare a playbook and a renewal decision, approve twice, complete with and without evidence, refresh the account and attempt approval of a stale proposal; one task and one decision exist, the stale approval returns 409 and the earlier decision keeps its original renewal date.

- **IMPORT-001 — Reviewed move-in custody.** Every import, including each incumbent migration in MIGRATE-001 to MIGRATE-005, runs through Base import-kit: a dry run, a reviewed plan fingerprint that apply must match, an idempotent operation key, and a journal with strict undo that survives restart. Undo refuses accounts edited since import or with dependent work. **Acceptance evidence:** Preview a JSON account file, apply with a wrong and then the reviewed fingerprint, replay the same operation key, undo an unchanged import, and attempt undo after preparing work on an imported account; the wrong fingerprint and the dependent undo return 409 and the replay returns the original job.

- **ERASE-001 — Owner erasure with tombstones.** Owner erasure, confirmed by typing the account name, atomically removes the account, its work, decisions and native and import custody; permanent target and source tombstones prevent replay into that installation. Copies held by connected systems are listed on the erasure receipt for the owner to action (ERASE-002); Buoy does not claim to delete them. **Acceptance evidence:** As a member, attempt erasure and receive 403; as the owner, erase an imported account with work, then read state, the import journal and history; nothing names the account, and re-creating the same ID returns 409.

- **RESTORE-001 — Portable installation archives.** Local RER archives carry domain data and import custody into an empty destination. New owner authentication is required; credentials, agent tokens, connector secrets, the outbox and settings are excluded. Corrupt and populated restores fail. Archives scale with SCALE-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Export an installation with accounts, approved decisions, an agent token and an erased account; restore into an empty copy, then attempt a second restore and a damaged archive; state matches, the agent token is refused, the tombstone holds and both bad restores fail.

- **SITE-001 — Public run site.** The static run site contains the product homepage, `/run`, Markdown owner guides and a 404. It reads no installation data and links only public routes. Installed `/` keeps the shared Lobby. Run-site deployment stays gated off until its release gate. **Acceptance evidence:** Build with `rer build --site` and crawl the output through Cloudflare's static-asset server; the homepage reaches `/run` and `/docs`, every guide renders and unknown paths return the 404.

- **STYLE-001 — One theme at every width.** The site, auth, setup and product share self-hosted Spectral and Red Hat Text and the paper, ink and signal tokens. Real forms, role boundaries, checklist content and evidence remain available at desktop and phone widths. **Acceptance evidence:** Open setup, sign-in, the account list, an account and the import screen at 1440 and 390 pixels; fonts load from the installation, no content or control is clipped and every form remains usable.

## Accounts, people and the customer record

- **ACCOUNT-001 — Account hierarchy, people and ownership.** Parent organisations, accounts and end users form one hierarchy, with rollups of ARR, users and health from children to parents. Each account holds stakeholders with roles (champion, executive sponsor, economic buyer, admin, billing contact, end user), the assigned CSM, account executive, executive sponsor and support owner, a lifecycle stage, a tier and custom fields. Contacts can belong to several accounts. **Acceptance evidence:** Through the account door and API, build a parent with two child accounts and 40 users, assign stakeholders and owners, and read the parent; rollups equal the sum of children, a contact appears on both accounts it belongs to and a member cannot reassign ownership.

- **ACCOUNT-002 — Unified customer timeline.** Each account and contact has one chronological timeline of emails, meetings, calls, notes, support tickets, invoices and payments, usage milestones, survey responses, playbook steps, tasks, approvals and renewal events. Every entry links to its source record and system and can be filtered by kind, person and source. Notes support @mentions and attachments. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a CRM, a billing source, a support source and an inbox in a test workspace, generate one record of each kind, and open the account timeline; every entry appears once, in order, with a working source link, and filters return only the chosen kinds.

- **ACCOUNT-003 — Workspaces, lists and saved views.** Each CSM has a home showing today's tasks, alerts, renewals and meetings for their book. Account and user lists support filters on any field or metric, chosen columns, sorting, bulk actions (assign, enrol in a playbook, add to segment), and personal or shared saved views. A manager can see any CSM's book. **Acceptance evidence:** As a CSM, save a filtered view of at-risk accounts renewing in 90 days, share it, bulk-assign three accounts and enrol them in a playbook; a second CSM sees the shared view, the assignments and enrolments are recorded once each.

- **ACCOUNT-004 — Custom fields, calculated fields and field history.** Admins add typed custom fields to accounts, users, contacts and opportunities, and calculated fields with a plain formula language (arithmetic, conditions, rollups, date differences) that previews against real accounts before saving and explains each result. Every field change keeps who, when, old and new value and source. **Acceptance evidence:** Add a picklist, a currency field and a formula field "seats used ÷ seats purchased", preview it on ten accounts, save, change an input and read the field history; the formula updates, its explanation shows the inputs and the history records both changes with actor and source.

- **ACCOUNT-005 — Search.** One search finds accounts, contacts, users, timeline entries, tasks, plans and opportunities by name, domain, email, external ID and text, respecting the reader's permissions. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed 5,000 accounts, search by a contact email, a Salesforce ID and a phrase from an email body; each result appears within one second and a restricted read-only user does not see accounts outside their scope.

## Connected data

- **CONNECT-001 — Connector framework, freshness and reconnection.** Every connector authenticates with OAuth or a scoped key held encrypted (SECURE-002), syncs on webhooks where the provider offers them and on a schedule otherwise, and records per-run counts, cursor, errors and last success. A connections page shows each source's freshness; a disconnected or failing source notifies its owner and admins, rechecks itself, and lowers the confidence of every health score, alert and forecast that depends on it. Nothing is presented as current when its source is stale. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a provider sandbox, run a sync, then revoke the grant and let a run fail; the connection shows the failure and last success, the owner receives a reconnection notice, affected accounts show reduced confidence naming the source, and reconnecting resumes from the cursor without duplicates.

- **IDENTITY-001 — Identity reconciliation and source authority.** Accounts, contacts and users are matched across systems by external IDs (Salesforce, HubSpot, Stripe, Zendesk, Intercom, Segment group IDs), domain and email, with a review queue for ambiguous matches and an undoable merge. Admins choose, per field, which system is authoritative; writes from a non-authoritative source are recorded but do not overwrite. **Acceptance evidence:** Sync overlapping accounts from Salesforce, Stripe and Zendesk where one company appears under two domains; exact ID matches link automatically, the ambiguous pair waits in review, a reviewed merge combines timelines, and undo restores both; an ARR field set to billing-authoritative ignores a conflicting CRM value and shows both.

- **CRM-001 — Salesforce two-way sync.** Accounts, contacts, users, opportunities (including renewal and expansion opportunities), owners and mapped custom fields sync from Salesforce. Buoy writes back health score, health colour, CSM, lifecycle stage, renewal risk and selected fields to mapped Salesforce fields. Field mapping, sync direction per field, conflict handling and API-limit pacing are visible and editable. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a Salesforce developer org, map fields, sync 1,000 accounts and their opportunities, change an account in each system, and run a writeback; Salesforce shows Buoy's health score and CSM, the conflicting edit follows the authority rule, and the sync log shows API usage under the configured limit.

- **CRM-002 — HubSpot two-way sync.** Companies, contacts, deals, owners and custom properties sync from HubSpot, with the same writeback of health, CSM, stage and renewal risk to HubSpot properties, per-field direction and conflict rules as CRM-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a HubSpot developer account, sync companies, contacts and deals, change a property in each system and write back health; HubSpot shows the Buoy properties, the conflict follows the authority rule and deal stage changes appear on the Buoy timeline.

- **CRM-003 — Orbit native link.** Buoy receives Orbit's post-sale handoff (Orbit INTEGRATE-008): account and contact identity, purchased products, commercial commitments, sales evidence references and the receiving owner, under a stable handoff ID that Buoy accepts or refuses with an actionable reason and reconciles on replay. Permitted account context (health, CSM, renewal date, expansion signals) flows back to Orbit. CS-qualified expansion leads become Orbit deals (RENEW-002). **Acceptance evidence:** Win a USD deal in Orbit, approve the handoff, and open the new Buoy account with its commitments and owner; replay the handoff and get one account; raise an expansion signal and see the Orbit deal; revoke the link and see a visible failure on both sides.

- **BILLING-001 — Stripe billing connector.** Stripe customers, subscriptions, subscription items, prices, invoices, payments, refunds, credit notes, failed payments and plan changes sync by webhook with periodic reconciliation. Buoy computes per-account MRR and ARR from active subscriptions (BILLING-003) and shows failed and overdue payments as risk signals. Buoy reads; it never charges, refunds or changes a subscription. **Acceptance evidence:** In Stripe test mode, create customers with monthly and annual subscriptions, an upgrade, a downgrade, a failed payment and a cancellation; Buoy's ARR per account equals the Stripe-derived figure, the failed payment raises a risk signal on the right account, and no write call reaches Stripe.

- **BILLING-002 — Chargebee, Nectar and QuickBooks Online revenue sources.** Chargebee and Nectar subscriptions, invoices and payments sync with the same model and guarantees as BILLING-001. Recurring-service companies that bill by invoice can use QuickBooks Online recurring invoices and payments (or Pantry) as the revenue source. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a Chargebee test site, a Nectar installation and a QuickBooks Online sandbox, each with renewals, a downgrade and an overdue invoice; each account's ARR and overdue balance match the source, with the source named on every figure.

- **BILLING-003 — Subscription financial model.** Each account has contracted ARR and MRR, billed revenue, products and seats purchased, entitlements, contract start and end, billing frequency, and a monthly ARR history classified as new, expansion, contraction, churn and reactivation. Accounts without a billing system use confirmed contract terms entered by a person, labelled as such. Every figure links to the billing records that produced it, and mismatches between contract and billing appear in a reconciliation queue. **Acceptance evidence:** With BILLING-001 data covering twelve months of upgrades, downgrades and one churn, open an account's revenue tab; each month's movement is classified correctly and links to its invoice or subscription change, and a contract-versus-billing mismatch appears in the queue until a person resolves it.

- **SUPPORT-001 — Zendesk connector.** Zendesk organisations, users, tickets, priorities, statuses, SLA breaches, satisfaction ratings and tags sync to accounts and contacts. Ticket volume, open urgent tickets, breached SLAs, CSAT and time to resolution become health measures and timeline entries; a CSM can open the ticket in Zendesk from Buoy. **Acceptance evidence:** In a Zendesk sandbox, create urgent, breached and solved tickets with CSAT for two organisations; Buoy shows each on the right account timeline, the health measures update on the next run and the source link opens the ticket.

- **SUPPORT-002 — Intercom and HappyToHelp connectors.** Intercom companies, contacts, conversations, tickets and ratings, and HappyToHelp customers, conversations, outcomes and CSAT, sync with the same measures and timeline entries as SUPPORT-001. HappyToHelp also receives Buoy's account context (health, tier, CSM, renewal date) for its agents. **Acceptance evidence:** With an Intercom test workspace and a HappyToHelp installation, open and resolve conversations for two accounts; Buoy shows them with ratings and HappyToHelp shows the Buoy health and CSM on the customer.

- **USAGE-001 — Segment and event API ingestion.** Product events arrive from Segment (track, identify, group) and from Buoy's own authenticated event API and JavaScript and server SDKs, deduplicated by message ID and mapped to users and accounts by user and group IDs. Unmapped events wait in a review queue rather than being dropped. **Acceptance evidence:** Send 100,000 Segment events across 50 accounts including duplicates and events for an unknown group; Buoy counts each event once, attributes them to the right users and accounts, and lists the unknown group for mapping.

- **USAGE-002 — Warehouse sync with Snowflake and BigQuery.** Admins connect Snowflake or BigQuery with a read-only role and schedule SQL queries that return account, user, usage or custom metrics keyed by an external ID; results load incrementally with row counts and failures shown. Buoy can also export its own accounts, health history, tasks and renewals to the warehouse. **Acceptance evidence:** Against a Snowflake trial and a BigQuery sandbox, schedule a daily usage query and an export; the metric appears per account with its run history, a broken query shows its error without erasing yesterday's values, and the exported tables contain current health and renewals.

- **USAGE-003 — Amplitude and Mixpanel connectors.** Product analytics cohorts, events and user properties from Amplitude and Mixpanel map to Buoy users and accounts with the guarantees of USAGE-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect an Amplitude and a Mixpanel test project, sync a feature event and a cohort, and see both attributed to the right accounts.

- **ADOPT-001 — Adoption and license metrics.** From usage data Buoy computes daily, weekly and monthly active users, feature and module adoption (defined by admins as event sets), depth and breadth of use, license utilisation against seats purchased, trend versus the previous period, and time to first value. Each metric is available per user, account and segment and can drive health, alerts, playbooks and reports. **Acceptance evidence:** With 90 days of synthetic events for 30 accounts, define two features and a seat count; Buoy's active-user, adoption and utilisation figures match an independent calculation from the same events, and a 30% drop in weekly active users is flagged as a trend.

- **PRODUCT-001 — Product issues and feature requests linked to accounts.** CSMs link an account to engineering issues in Jira, Linear or Vector and to feature requests in Penny or Productboard, from Buoy or from the support ticket. Each issue shows the linked accounts and their combined ARR and renewal dates; status changes flow back to the account timeline, and when an issue ships the owning CSMs get a task to tell their customers. **Acceptance evidence:** Link three accounts to one Jira issue and one to a Penny request, then move the issue to done; the issue shows the three accounts' ARR, each timeline records the status change, and each CSM receives the follow-up task once.

## Health, segments and signals

- **HEALTH-001 — Configurable, explainable health scores.** Admins build scorecards from weighted measures (usage, adoption, support, billing, survey, engagement, CSM sentiment and any custom or calculated field) with thresholds per measure, overall bands and different weights per segment. Missing data is treated explicitly per measure (excluded and reweighted, or scored as unknown) and the result says so. Scores recalculate at least hourly and on source change, keep history, and every score explains which measures moved it and from which source. A scorecard change previews against current and historical accounts before it is published, and is versioned. **Acceptance evidence:** Build a scorecard with five measures and segment-specific weights, preview it, publish it, then remove a source; every account shows a score with its measure breakdown and sources, history shows the change of version, and the missing source is reweighted or shown as unknown exactly as configured.

- **HEALTH-002 — CSM sentiment and manual measures.** A CSM can record a sentiment rating with a reason that feeds the scorecard, expires after a set period and is visible as a person's judgment rather than data. **Acceptance evidence:** Record a red sentiment with a reason, observe the score change and explanation, then advance past expiry; the measure becomes missing and the explanation says it expired.

- **SIGNAL-001 — Risk and opportunity alerts.** Admins define signals from any field, metric, event or change: usage drop, champion or sponsor left, ticket spike, SLA breach, failed payment, detractor survey response, renewal approaching without engagement, license utilisation above a threshold, new department adoption or a positive survey. Signals are deduplicated, routed to the account owner and chosen channels (in-app, email, Slack, Teams), can be snoozed or dismissed with a reason, and can start playbooks. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure a usage-drop risk and a 90% utilisation upsell signal, generate both, and dismiss one with a reason; each fires once per account, reaches the owner in-app and in Slack, the dismissed one does not re-fire inside its window and the upsell signal can open an expansion opportunity.

- **SEGMENT-001 — Segments, tiers and lifecycle stages.** Dynamic segments defined by rules over any field, metric or relationship update automatically and record entry and exit times; static lists are also available. Accounts carry a service tier (for example high-touch, scaled, digital) and a lifecycle stage (onboarding, adoption, expansion, renewal, churned) set by rule or by hand. Segments drive scorecard weights, playbooks, journeys, permissions and reports. **Acceptance evidence:** Define a segment "ARR over $50,000 and health below 60", change an account so it enters and leaves, and run a report filtered by the segment; entry and exit are timestamped, a playbook triggered on entry runs once and the report counts match the rule.

## Lifecycle work

- **PLAY-001 — Automated playbooks.** Playbooks start on a signal, segment entry, lifecycle stage change, a date relative to renewal, contract start or onboarding milestone, a webhook, or by hand. Steps create tasks, send or draft emails (EMAIL-001), post to Slack or Teams, wait for a time or condition, branch on data, update fields, start another playbook, call a webhook (or a Crank flow) or pause for approval (APPROVAL-001). Each playbook has entry and exit criteria, re-enrolment limits, owners and per-step due dates, and stops when its exit criteria are met. **Acceptance evidence:** Build a playbook triggered by health falling below 50 with a task, a wait, a branch on whether usage recovered, an approval-gated email and a Slack post; push three accounts through both branches and a fourth that recovers mid-way; steps run in order, the recovered account exits, no account enrols twice inside the limit and every step is on the timeline.

- **PLAY-002 — Tasks, reminders and escalation.** Tasks (Gainsight calls them CTAs) have an owner, due date, priority, type and linked account, plan or opportunity. Owners get reminders before and on the due date; overdue tasks escalate to the owner's manager after a configured delay; tasks move to the new owner when an account is reassigned or the owner is away. Completing a task records an outcome reason and evidence. **Acceptance evidence:** Create tasks due tomorrow and yesterday, reassign an account, and complete one with an outcome; reminders arrive in-app and by email, the overdue task escalates to the manager, the reassigned task moves and the completed task stores outcome and evidence.

- **PLAY-003 — Automation run history and failure alerts.** Every playbook, journey and connector run is logged with its inputs, step results and errors; admins set failure thresholds and are notified in-app, by email and in Slack when they are crossed, and failed steps can be retried after a fix without repeating completed ones. **Acceptance evidence:** Break a webhook step, run a playbook for ten accounts, cross the threshold, fix the step and retry; admins are notified once, the log shows the failures, and retry completes only the failed steps.

- **PLAY-004 — Outcome tracking for playbooks.** Each playbook records which enrolled accounts reached its intended outcome (health recovered, milestone reached, renewal closed) within a window, with completion rates, time to outcome and comparison to unenrolled accounts in the same segment. **Acceptance evidence:** Run a playbook for 40 accounts with a defined outcome, let half reach it, and open the playbook report; outcome rate, time to outcome and the unenrolled comparison are shown with their account lists.

- **ONBOARD-001 — Onboarding projects.** Templates for onboarding and implementation projects hold phases, milestones and tasks for both internal team members and customer contacts, with dependencies, relative due dates, owners and target go-live. A project starts automatically on a new account or Orbit handoff, tracks percent complete, time to value and slippage, flags overdue customer-side tasks and rolls status up to the account and onboarding reports. **Acceptance evidence:** Start a project from a template on an Orbit handoff, complete internal tasks, leave a customer task overdue and shift a dependency; dates recalculate, the project shows at risk with the overdue customer task named, and the onboarding report shows time to value per account.

- **PLAN-001 — Shared success plans and goals.** Success plans record the customer's business objectives, the measures that prove them (linked to usage, survey or custom metrics, or a confirmed customer statement), milestones and owners on both sides. Plans can be shared with customer contacts (PORTAL-001) and are reviewed at business reviews; goal attainment feeds health and renewal readiness. **Acceptance evidence:** Create a plan with two objectives, one tied to a usage metric and one confirmed by the customer, share it with the champion, and let the metric reach its target; the first objective shows achieved from data, the second waits for the customer's confirmation and health shows the goal measure.

- **PORTAL-001 — Customer collaboration portal.** Invited customer contacts sign in by email link (or their company SSO) to a portal scoped to their own account, where they see and update their onboarding tasks and success plan, comment, upload files and confirm outcomes. Portal users never see internal notes, health scores or other customers. **Acceptance evidence:** Invite a champion, complete a customer task and comment from the portal, then attempt to reach another account's portal URL and an internal note; the task and comment appear on the internal timeline and both cross-boundary reads are refused.

- **REVIEW-001 — Business review preparation and decks.** For a QBR or EBR Buoy assembles the account's outcomes, adoption, support, survey and renewal facts into an editable deck from a template, exportable to PDF, PowerPoint and Google Slides, with every chart linked to its data. A person reviews and edits before anything reaches the customer, and the review and its follow-ups are logged. **Acceptance evidence:** Generate a QBR deck for an account with 90 days of data, edit a slide, export to PPTX and PDF and log the meeting; figures match the account's reports and the follow-up tasks appear on the timeline.

## Communication

- **EMAIL-001 — Two-way email through Gmail and Microsoft 365.** CSMs connect their Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 mailbox. Emails with known contacts log to the account timeline (with per-user exclusion of private threads); CSMs send from Buoy through their own mailbox with templates and merge fields, and replies thread back. Shared team templates and snippets are versioned. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a Google Workspace and a Microsoft 365 test mailbox, send a templated email from Buoy, reply from the customer address and send a private personal email; the sent and reply messages appear threaded on the timeline, merge fields render and the private email does not appear.

- **EMAIL-002 — Sending controls and consent.** Automated and one-to-many sends respect recipient-time-zone send windows, per-mailbox and per-day limits, a CAN-SPAM unsubscribe and postal address for non-transactional messages, a suppression list, bounce handling, and stop-on-reply. Playbook and agent emails can pause for human review before sending; AI drafts are always labelled until a person approves them. **Acceptance evidence:** Schedule a journey to 200 contacts across four US time zones with a daily cap, unsubscribe one, bounce one and reply from one; sends land inside each window and under the cap, the unsubscribed and bounced contacts receive nothing further, the replier stops and drafts wait for approval where configured.

- **JOURNEY-001 — Digital customer success journeys.** For scaled and digital tiers, admins build multi-step journeys to users and contacts: emails, in-app messages (INAPP-001), waits, branches on behaviour and survey answers, and handoff to a human task when a branch needs it. Journeys use segments, honour EMAIL-002, report delivery, opens, clicks, replies and goal conversion, and stop when the account's lifecycle changes. Promotional newsletters stay in Mango; journeys are lifecycle communication to existing customers. **Acceptance evidence:** Run an onboarding journey to 500 users with a branch on feature adoption, and change one account to churned mid-journey; branches follow behaviour, the churned account exits, the report counts match the delivery log and a branch that needs a human creates a task.

- **INAPP-001 — In-app messages and guides.** A JavaScript snippet in the buyer's product shows segmented announcements, banners, checklists and walkthroughs to identified users, records views, completions and dismissals as usage, and respects frequency caps. **Acceptance evidence:** Install the snippet on a test app, target a walkthrough to one segment and a banner with a frequency cap, and visit as users in and out of the segment; only targeted users see each, caps hold and completions appear on user timelines.

- **MEET-001 — Calendar capture.** Google Calendar and Outlook Calendar events with customer contacts log as meetings on the account timeline, upcoming meetings show on the CSM home with a preparation brief (AI-001), and CSMs can share a Dial or Calendly booking link in emails and journeys. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect both calendars, create a past and a future meeting with a customer contact and an internal-only meeting; the customer meetings appear on the account, the future one on the home with a brief, and the internal one does not appear.

- **MEET-002 — Call recordings and transcripts.** Recordings and transcripts from Gong, Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams attach to the meeting on the timeline, with consent status recorded; summaries, proposed tasks and risk or expansion mentions follow AI-002. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a Zoom transcript and a Gong call for one account; both attach to the right meetings and their summaries cite transcript lines.

- **CHAT-001 — Slack, Microsoft Teams and Nook alerts and actions.** Signals, approvals, overdue tasks and daily digests post to chosen Slack, Teams or Nook channels or direct messages; recipients can acknowledge, snooze, complete a task or approve from the message where their role permits; a command looks up an account's health, owner and renewal. Messages in shared customer channels (Slack Connect) can log to the timeline when an admin enables it. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect a Slack workspace and a Teams tenant, fire a signal and an approval, approve from Slack as an admin and attempt the same as a member, and run the account lookup; the admin's approval records once with their identity, the member is refused and the lookup returns current health.

- **SURVEY-001 — NPS and CSAT relationship surveys.** Buoy sends NPS and CSAT surveys by email and in-app on schedules per contact or after events (onboarding complete, ticket solved), with throttling so no contact is over-surveyed. Responses land on the contact and account timeline, feed health, and start playbooks for detractors and promoters. Custom research surveys are built in Pencil, Typeform or SurveyMonkey and their responses import with the same mapping. **Acceptance evidence:** Survey 300 contacts with a 90-day throttle, answer as a detractor, a passive and a promoter, and import a Pencil survey; NPS computes correctly by segment, the detractor starts a playbook, the throttle blocks a second send and the Pencil responses appear on the right contacts.

- **NOTIFY-001 — In-app notifications and digests.** Each user has an inbox of mentions, assignments, alerts and approvals, and an optional daily or weekly email digest of their book's changes, with per-type preferences. **Acceptance evidence:** Mention a user, assign a task and fire a signal, then set preferences to digest-only for signals; the inbox shows all three and the next digest includes the signal but no duplicate notification.

## Renewals, expansion and revenue

- **RENEW-001 — Renewal pipeline.** Each contract or subscription generates a renewal opportunity a configured number of days before its end date, carrying expected ARR, the notice deadline (from confirmed terms, as today), owner, stage, forecast category (commit, likely, at risk) and risk reasons. Outcomes are renewed, renewed with expansion, renewed with contraction, churned or deferred, each with a reason code and evidence, recorded through APPROVAL-001. A renewal board and list show every renewal by quarter. **Acceptance evidence:** With twelve subscriptions ending across two quarters, let Buoy create renewals, move stages, record one churn with a reason and one contraction; each renewal appears once at the right lead time, notice dates match terms, outcomes need approval and the ARR change matches billing after the next sync.

- **RENEW-002 — Expansion pipeline and CS-qualified leads.** CSMs and signals open expansion opportunities (upsell, cross-sell, seats, new team) with expected ARR and stage. A qualified lead is handed to the sales system of record (Orbit, Salesforce or HubSpot) as an opportunity with its context, and stage and amount sync back so CS-sourced expansion is credited. **Acceptance evidence:** Open an expansion from a utilisation signal, hand it to Salesforce and to Orbit, then win it in each; both opportunities exist once with Buoy's context, and the closed amounts appear as CS-sourced expansion in Buoy reports.

- **RENEW-003 — CRM renewal opportunity sync.** Renewal opportunities are created in or matched to Salesforce or HubSpot renewal opportunities and kept in sync both ways for stage, amount, close date and owner, under the source-authority rules of IDENTITY-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Create renewals in Buoy for accounts whose renewal opportunities already exist in Salesforce and HubSpot; Buoy matches rather than duplicates, a stage change in either system appears in the other and a conflicting amount follows the authority rule.

- **RENEW-004 — Renewal forecast.** A forecast by month and quarter, by CSM, team and segment, shows expected, commit and at-risk ARR, best and worst case, and the change since the last weekly snapshot; managers can override a forecast category with a reason. Closed periods compare forecast with actual. **Acceptance evidence:** Snapshot a quarter's forecast, move three renewals and close them, and open the forecast; it shows the movement since the snapshot, the override with its reason and forecast versus actual for the closed month.

- **RENEW-005 — Renewal paperwork and terms.** Confirmed contract terms (dates, notice period, auto-renew, price uplift, termination rights) can come from Locket, Ironclad or uploaded contracts as well as by hand, each with its evidence. Renewal order forms or quotes produced in Orbit, Salesforce or the buyer's quote tool go out for signature through Inkwell or DocuSign, and the signed status and document link return to the renewal. **Acceptance evidence:** Import terms from a Locket contract, send a renewal order through Inkwell and through DocuSign sandbox, and sign both; the renewal shows each term with its source, the signed status and the document link.

- **REPORT-001 — NRR, GRR and retention reporting.** Net revenue retention, gross revenue retention and logo retention use stated definitions (starting ARR cohort for the period, plus expansion, minus contraction and churn, excluding new logos) and are reported by month, quarter and trailing twelve months, by segment, CSM, cohort, plan and tier. Every figure drills down to accounts and to the invoices or subscription changes behind them, and reconciles to the billing source; unreconciled differences appear as exceptions, never as adjustments. **Acceptance evidence:** With a year of Stripe data, compute NRR and GRR for a quarter and compare with an independent spreadsheet calculation from the same Stripe export; the figures match to the cent, drill-down lists each account's movement, and an injected unmatched invoice appears as an exception.

- **REPORT-002 — Dashboards and report builder.** Prebuilt dashboards cover portfolio health distribution and trend, renewals and forecast, NRR and GRR, onboarding time to value, adoption, NPS, task and playbook completion and CSM book size. Users build reports on any object with filters, groupings, charts and calculated columns whose formulas are shown; dashboards are shared by role and delivered on a schedule by email or Slack; every report exports to CSV. **Acceptance evidence:** Build a report of at-risk ARR by CSM, add it to a dashboard, schedule it weekly to Slack and export it; the scheduled post and CSV match the on-screen figures and a read-only viewer can open but not edit the dashboard.

## Team, roles and administration

- **ROLE-001 — Roles and permissions.** Roles are owner, admin, CS manager, CSM, read-only (for sales, support, finance and executives) and scoped agent, with record scope by book, team or segment and field-level restriction for commercial fields. Approval rights for concessions and renewal outcomes are assignable. Customer portal users are a separate class (PORTAL-001). **Acceptance evidence:** As each role, open accounts inside and outside scope, edit a commercial field, approve a renewal outcome and invite a user; each succeeds or is refused exactly as the role matrix states, and every refusal is server-side.

- **ROLE-002 — Single sign-on and provisioning.** Staff sign in with Google Workspace, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta or Auth over OIDC or SAML, with optional enforcement. SCIM provisions and deprovisions users and maps groups to roles; deprovisioning revokes sessions and agent tokens they issued. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure Okta with SAML and SCIM, sign in, change a user's group, then deprovision them; their role follows the group, their session ends and their agent tokens are revoked.

- **TEAM-001 — Books of business and reassignment.** Accounts are assigned to CSMs by rule (segment, territory, ARR band, round-robin) or by hand, with pods and teams, capacity targets per CSM and a reassignment flow that moves open tasks, plans and journeys and posts a handover note to the timeline. **Acceptance evidence:** Assign 300 accounts by rule across four CSMs with capacity targets, then reassign one CSM's book; counts respect targets, every open task and plan moves and each account shows the handover note.

- **AUDIT-001 — Durable audit log.** Sign-ins, role changes, data exports, erasures, approvals, connector changes, scorecard and playbook publishes, external sends and every agent action record actor, time, action and an opaque subject reference. The audit survives erasure of its subject with personal details removed, is searchable by admins and exports with the archive. **Acceptance evidence:** Perform each action kind, erase an account and its contact, then read and export the audit; every action is listed, entries for the erased account remain without its name, and the export matches the screen.

- **SECURE-002 — Connector credential custody.** OAuth tokens and keys for every connector are encrypted at rest, scoped to the least access the connector needs, visible to admins by name and scope only, revocable in one action, excluded from archives and never sent to models or agents. **Acceptance evidence:** Connect Salesforce, Stripe and Slack, export an archive, read the connections page as an admin and an agent, and revoke one; no secret appears in the archive, page or agent response, and the revoked connector stops on its next run.

- **ERASE-002 — Contact-level privacy requests.** For a CCPA/CPRA or contractual request, an owner can erase or export one contact or end user across Buoy (timeline, survey responses, usage events, emails) with a receipt, while the account remains. The receipt lists connected systems that still hold that person's data so the owner can action them there. **Acceptance evidence:** Erase a contact with emails, a survey response and usage events, then read the account, reports and audit; nothing names the contact, aggregates stay correct, and the receipt lists Salesforce and Zendesk as external holders.

- **API-001 — Public API and webhooks.** A documented, versioned REST API (OpenAPI) with scoped tokens covers accounts, contacts, users, events, metrics, tasks, plans, opportunities and timeline entries, with pagination, idempotency keys and rate limits. Outbound signed webhooks fire on health changes, signals, playbook steps, renewal changes and survey responses, with retries and a delivery log. Zapier, Make and Crank use the same API. **Acceptance evidence:** With a scoped token, create and update an account and post events, replay with the same idempotency key, exceed the rate limit, and subscribe a webhook to health changes; writes happen once, the limit returns 429 with retry timing and each webhook delivery is signed and logged.

- **MIGRATE-001 — Gainsight CS migration.** Buoy imports Gainsight companies, relationships, people, scorecards and measures, CTAs and tasks, playbooks, success plans, timeline activities, NPS responses and renewal data from Gainsight's exports and API, maps them to Buoy objects with a reviewed plan (IMPORT-001), reports what could not be mapped, and can run in parallel with Gainsight until cutover. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a Gainsight sample export with 500 companies and their objects; the preview shows counts per object and unmapped fields, apply creates matching records, a sample of 20 accounts reconciles field by field and undo removes the import.

- **MIGRATE-002 — ChurnZero migration.** Accounts, contacts, ChurnScores and their factors, tasks, plays, success plans, journeys, events and NPS import from ChurnZero exports and API under the same reviewed plan and reconciliation as MIGRATE-001. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a ChurnZero sample export, reconcile 20 accounts and their health factors field by field, and confirm journeys arrive as paused Buoy journeys for review.

- **MIGRATE-003 — Totango migration.** Accounts, users, health profiles, SuccessFlows, tasks, touchpoints, segments and success plays import from Totango exports and API under the same guarantees. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a Totango sample export, reconcile 20 accounts, and confirm SuccessFlows arrive as paused playbooks with their steps mapped or listed as unmapped.

- **MIGRATE-004 — Planhat migration.** Companies, end users, health profiles, workflows and tasks, conversations, licenses and revenue, NPS and custom fields import from Planhat's API and exports under the same guarantees. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a Planhat sample export, reconcile 20 accounts including license ARR, and confirm custom fields arrive with their types.

- **MIGRATE-005 — Vitally, Velaris and spreadsheet migration.** Vitally and Velaris accounts, users, health, projects, tasks, notes and NPS import from their exports and APIs, and any CSV or Google Sheets book of business imports through a guided column mapper, under the same guarantees. **Acceptance evidence:** Import a Vitally sample export and a 2,000-row CSV with messy dates and currencies; the mapper flags bad rows without importing them and the rest reconcile.

- **MOBILE-001 — Mobile apps.** Native iOS and Android apps give CSMs their home, account lookup with health and timeline, tasks, approvals, alerts by push notification and voice or text notes after a meeting; recent accounts are readable offline and changes made offline sync with conflict notice. The web app remains fully usable at phone width (STYLE-001). **Acceptance evidence:** On iOS and Android test devices, receive a push alert, approve a renewal outcome as an admin, dictate a meeting note offline and reconnect; the approval records once, the note syncs to the timeline and a conflicting edit shows a notice.

- **A11Y-001 — Accessibility.** Product, portal and mobile screens meet WCAG 2.2 AA: keyboard operation, visible focus, labelled controls, text alternatives for charts and colour never the only signal of health. **Acceptance evidence:** Run axe and a keyboard-only pass over the home, account, playbook builder, report and portal screens; no serious violations, every chart has a text table and every health colour has a text label.

- **SCALE-001 — Scale for a growing book.** One installation handles 10,000 accounts, 500,000 end users and 50 million usage events a month with hourly health recalculation, account pages under two seconds and reports under ten. Today's 500-account guard is lifted only when this holds. **Acceptance evidence:** Load the stated volumes into a test installation and measure health recalculation, account page and report timings, and archive export and restore; all meet the targets and no import or archive truncates silently.

## AI assistance

- **AI-001 — Cited account summaries and questions.** A CSM can ask about an account or the portfolio ("why is Harbour at risk?", "which renewals next quarter have no executive contact?") and get an answer that cites the timeline entries, metrics and records it used, says what it could not see, and respects the asker's permissions. Meeting preparation briefs use the same method. **Acceptance evidence:** Ask five scripted questions over a seeded portfolio as a CSM and as a read-only user; every claim has a working citation, missing data is named, and the read-only user's answer excludes out-of-scope accounts.

- **AI-002 — Conversation and meeting summaries with proposed work.** Emails, meetings, transcripts, chats and tickets are summarised with cited lines, and proposed tasks, plan updates, risk mentions and expansion mentions wait for a person to accept or discard them. **Acceptance evidence:** Process a transcript with a commitment, a competitor mention and an expansion request; three proposals appear with citations, accepting one creates the task and discarding one leaves no record but the audit.

- **AI-003 — Sentiment and risk from communications.** Buoy scores sentiment and detects risk themes (frustration, competitor, budget, champion change) in customer communications as an explainable health measure that cites the messages behind it and can be disabled per data class. **Acceptance evidence:** Process a month of synthetic emails and tickets for ten accounts with known sentiment; the measure ranks them consistently with the labels, each score cites messages and disabling the measure removes it from health with a visible note.

- **AI-004 — Owner-selected models and data controls.** The owner chooses the model provider (Anthropic, OpenAI or a compatible endpoint) and which data classes (emails, transcripts, tickets, usage, revenue) may be sent, sees per-feature usage cost in USD, and can turn AI features off entirely; nothing is used for provider training under the configured terms. **Acceptance evidence:** Configure Anthropic, exclude transcripts, and run AI-001 and AI-002; requests use the chosen model, no transcript text leaves the installation, cost is shown per feature, and turning AI off hides AI features and stops requests.

## Push further: agents that carry the job

Agents act with GOVERN-001's scoped authority on the data named in each clause. People keep control of concessions, commitments, external promises and anything that changes money or a contract.

- **GOVERN-001 — Agent authority and action ledger.** Each agent has an owner-issued, expiring, revocable credential with named scopes (read, prepare, send within policy, update fields), a USD spend budget, rate limits and the data classes it may read. Agents may prepare anything in scope but cannot approve their own proposals; external sends follow EMAIL-002 policy and approval settings; concessions, discounts, credits, contract changes and product commitments always need a person with that approval right. Every agent read, proposal and action lands in an action ledger, and a kill switch stops all agents at once. **Acceptance evidence:** Issue a prepare-only agent and a send-within-policy agent, have each attempt preparation, approval, an in-policy email, an out-of-policy email and a discount; only allowed actions occur, approvals and the discount are refused, the ledger records every attempt, and the kill switch stops both mid-run.

- **AGENT-001 — Verified risk resolution.** For an at-risk account the agent tests whether the underlying problem is really unresolved by checking support tickets and their linked engineering issues (Jira, Linear, Vector), product usage of the affected feature, billing state and the latest customer messages. It proposes one accountable remedy with owners in support, product and commercial teams, follows each owner's step to completion, and only marks the risk resolved when the customer's data or own words confirm the outcome (usage restored, ticket confirmed fixed, customer reply). Unconfirmed benefit stays unconfirmed. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed an account whose urgent ticket is marked solved but whose feature usage is still zero and whose engineering issue is open; the agent reports the problem as unresolved with citations, proposes a remedy with three owners, chases overdue steps, and marks it resolved only after usage recovers and the customer confirms; the CSM approves the remedy and every message before it is sent.

- **AGENT-002 — Retention interventions measured against comparable accounts.** The agent proposes a small intervention for a segment (for example an onboarding call for accounts that stall at a milestone), and once CS leadership approves the experiment and its success metric, assigns comparable holdout accounts by segment, baseline health, ARR and tenure, runs the intervention through playbooks and reports the difference in outcome and renewal with its uncertainty, sample size and known biases. It never changes pricing or disadvantages a customer, and never claims a health increase proves retained revenue. **Acceptance evidence:** On a seeded history with a known effect, approve an experiment; the agent's holdout matches on the stated factors, the reported effect falls within the seeded range with an interval, and an experiment that would withhold a contracted service is refused.

- **AGENT-003 — Evidence-backed renewal readiness.** From 120 days before each renewal the agent reads terms, notice dates, billing, success-plan goals, stakeholder engagement and open risks; identifies missing renewal authority (no economic buyer contact, champion departed), unresolved obligations and unconfirmed goals; prepares a joint timeline with the customer; and after people approve commercial steps verifies that they reached the CRM and billing system. People confirm terms, amounts and negotiation positions; the agent cannot amend a contract, offer a discount or charge a card. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed renewals with a departed champion, an unconfirmed goal and an uplift not yet in Stripe; the agent flags each with citations, prepares the timeline for CSM approval and reports the Stripe mismatch until the subscription matches the approved terms.

- **AGENT-004 — Portfolio-wide causes of risk.** Across the accounts the requester may read, the agent clusters complaints, stalled onboarding milestones, support defects and churn reasons into shared causes, quantifies the ARR exposed, routes a proposed systemic fix to the product owner in Vector, Jira or Linear and the feature request to Penny, and after release checks which affected customers actually benefited. No customer's confidential detail appears in another customer's view or message. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed 200 accounts where 30 share a defect; the agent reports the cluster with its ARR and citations, files one linked issue after approval, and after the issue closes reports which of the 30 recovered; a CSM scoped to ten accounts sees only their own accounts in the cluster.

- **AGENT-005 — Scaled-tier digital CSM.** For digital and scaled accounts the agent monitors health, usage and lifecycle events, sends approved-template outreach within EMAIL-002 policy, answers customer replies that match approved knowledge, books meetings through Dial links when a human is needed, and escalates to a named CSM with a cited summary when risk, a commercial question or an unknown arises. **Acceptance evidence:** Run the agent over 300 scaled accounts for a simulated month with replies of each kind; in-policy outreach is sent, a pricing question and a cancellation threat escalate with summaries, no reply invents a commitment, and every message is in the ledger.

- **AGENT-006 — Onboarding follow-through.** The agent watches onboarding projects, reminds customer and internal owners of overdue tasks in their own channel (email, portal, Slack Connect) within policy, proposes rescheduled dates when a dependency slips, and tells the CSM when go-live is genuinely at risk. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed a project with an overdue customer task and a slipped dependency; reminders go to the right people within policy, the proposed new dates wait for CSM approval, and the at-risk alert cites the blocking tasks.

- **AGENT-007 — Data trust upkeep.** The agent watches connectors and joins: it proposes identity merges, flags accounts whose CRM, billing and support data disagree (ARR mismatch, a churned subscription with an active CRM status, users mapped to the wrong account), names stale sources behind health scores and opens fix tasks for the source owner. Merges and authority changes wait for an admin. **Acceptance evidence:** Seed a duplicated company, an ARR mismatch and a mis-mapped Segment group; the agent proposes each fix with evidence, applies only what an admin approves, and the affected health scores update with the change noted.

- **AGENT-008 — Plain-language configuration.** An admin describes a scorecard, segment, playbook or report in plain language; the agent builds a draft, back-tests scorecards and signals against the last twelve months (how often they would have fired, how many churned accounts they would have caught), shows the draft and test, and publishes only on approval. **Acceptance evidence:** Ask for "flag accounts whose weekly active users fall 30% and who have an open urgent ticket"; the draft signal back-tests on seeded history with fire counts and churn capture shown, and nothing is published until the admin approves.

## Honest boundaries

- **BOUNDARY-001 — Not a billing processor.** Buoy reads billing and never charges cards, collects ACH, runs dunning, calculates or remits sales tax or changes subscriptions. Buyers do that in Nectar, Stripe Billing, Chargebee or QuickBooks Online, which stay authoritative for invoices and cash; Buoy reconciles to them (REPORT-001) and verifies approved changes reached them (AGENT-003). **Acceptance evidence:** Inspect the connector scopes requested from Stripe and Chargebee and the API surface; only read scopes are requested and no route moves money.

- **BOUNDARY-002 — Not the sales CRM, help desk, product analytics or marketing email platform.** Buoy does not run new-business pipeline, support queues, event analytics dashboards or promotional newsletters. Buyers use Orbit, Salesforce or HubSpot; HappyToHelp, Zendesk or Intercom; Amplitude, Mixpanel or their warehouse; Mango or Mailchimp, through the connectors above. **Acceptance evidence:** Follow the documented setup for each sibling and incumbent connector from a fresh installation; each link works without rebuilding that product inside Buoy.

- **BOUNDARY-003 — No guaranteed prediction.** Health scores, forecasts and AI risk signals are explainable judgments over the data present, not guaranteed churn predictions or proof of causation. Missing data stays visible, forecasts compare with actual renewals (RENEW-004), and people decide interventions and commitments. **Acceptance evidence:** Read any health score, forecast and AI risk signal; each shows its inputs, missing sources and, for closed periods, its accuracy against actual outcomes.

- **BOUNDARY-004 — No bulk-mail network, consent bypass or autonomous commercial change.** Buoy sends through the buyer's own Google or Microsoft mailboxes (or a provider such as SendGrid for journeys) under their terms and US CAN-SPAM rules; it does not operate a sending network, buy lists or send to unsubscribed contacts, and no agent changes commercial terms. **Acceptance evidence:** Attempt a journey send to a suppressed contact and an agent discount; both are refused and recorded.

- **BOUNDARY-005 — Certifications are the deployment's.** Buoy is self-hosted on the buyer's Cloudflare account. SOC 2 or similar attestations cover the buyer's deployment and are prepared with Evidence or their auditor; a provider's network, terms and data-processing agreements still apply to each connector. **Acceptance evidence:** The owner guides list each connector's data flows and the provider terms that govern them.
